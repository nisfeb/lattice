How it works

One store on your ship, and every surface you would want on top of it. Publish a page and anyone on the network can read it instantly, straight from your ship. Save a memory and your assistant recalls it next session.

What it does

  • Pages that run Markdown, gemtext, HTML, JS, CSS. Or write a page in Hoon and it computes its content, with commands, state, and dependencies on other pages, live-previewed in the editor.
  • Knowledge & AI memory A private, tagged note store with history and trash. Browse it at /know. Your agents read and write the same store over MCP, served by the ship itself.
  • Search everything A full-text catalog over your pages and the ships you follow, right from the reader’s omnibar.
  • A real filesystem Mount your pages as local files with lattice-fs. A cold mount warms in one round-trip, so grep runs from RAM. Edit in your own editor and saves round-trip safely.
  • Browse urb:// Fetch and read pages published by any ship, peer-to-peer over Urbit remote scry. The bytes come straight from the author.
  • Publish Anything you publish is instantly readable by anyone as urb://~you/that/path. A page can also go clearweb-public, served over HTTP from your ship.
  • Follow & subscribe Follow ships to see what they publish. Subscribe to a file for a live push when it changes. Discover publishers via %contacts. Copy any remote file onto your own ship.
  • Web workspace A three-pane editor with tree, live preview, and drag-and-drop upload of files or whole directories. Installs as a PWA with the flat-green tile you are looking at.
  • Share whole sites Share a folder and everything under it goes clearweb-public at /c/<folder>/ with the theme inherited. A site, published and unpublished in one click.
  • Works offline Lose the ship mid-session and saves queue locally (pages and memories both), then replay on reconnect. Concurrent edits are never lost. The newest wins and the other is kept as a real page under conflicts/.
  • Bookmarks Star any urb:// page from the reader bar. The full list is organized into folders and searchable as you type. The omnibar ranks bookmarks above history.
  • Comments & moderation Readers can leave comments on pages you publish. An inbox in the workspace lists every comment and removes any of them in one click.
  • Groups & bans Named groups of ships with per-path read/edit grants, a full ACL editor, per-file sharing to a ship or a group, and a banlist that revokes on ban, not just labels.
  • Live location A template page that shares your position with chosen ships: a map with a trail of recent fixes, periodic auto-updates while open, and a hard expiry it enforces itself.

What it isn't

  • Not a host. Bring your own ship: yours, a friend's, or a hosted one.
  • Not the HTTP web. Pages are Urbit-native (urb://~ship/path) and move between ships over remote scry, not over DNS and HTTP.
  • Not an app you must install. The client is served by your ship. The desktop app and the filesystem client are optional local extras.

Privacy

The only server lattice talks to is your ship.

No analytics, no third-party SDKs, no telemetry. Every client speaks only to the Urbit ship you point it at. That's the entire network surface. Your session never leaves the device unencrypted. lattice refuses to send your +code or cookie in cleartext to a remote ship, and stores it owner-only at rest.

Everything you put in /pub is public by design. That's the whole point of a publishing tool. Nothing else on your ship is exposed.

Under the hood

On your ship, lattice runs as a nexus in the grubbery framework. Pages and knowledge live as grubs in the vault, published pages answer remote scry, and the MCP knowledge tools are compiled into the same ball they serve. The web client is itself hosted on lattice. The editor, reader, and PWA are grubs in the same ball they edit, over an authenticated HTTP API with live SSE updates. The filesystem client is Rust over a pluggable transport (HTTP or grubbery's local IPC). Hoon unit tests on the nexus, a 19-assertion ship-verified regression matrix on the filesystem client.

Get started

lattice hosts its own client, so there is nothing to download. Put the nexus on your ship (see the install notes), log in at your ship's web login, and open:

/apps/lattice The reader. Your pages, the urb:// omnibar, catalog search, ships you follow.
/apps/lattice/app The workspace. Editor with highlighting and live preview, sharing, uploads, knowledge browser.
desktop app Linux (deb, AppImage) and macOS (dmg, Apple Silicon + Intel). The ship-served client in its own window, plus managed filesystem mounts and pier auto-detection. Releases

On a phone, use your browser's Install app / Add to Home Screen. lattice is a full PWA with its own icon and standalone window. It resumes where you left off and keeps working offline. Prefer real files? Build the FUSE client (cargo build --release in lattice-fs-rs/) and mount your page tree, or nix build .#lattice-desktop.

New to Urbit? urbit.org/overview/running-urbit walks you through booting a ship. Once you have its HTTP URL and +code, log in and you're browsing.
