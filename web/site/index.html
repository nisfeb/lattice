How it works

One store on your ship, and every surface you would want on top of it. Publish a page and anyone on the network can read it — instantly, straight from your ship. Save a memory and your assistant recalls it next session.

What it does

  • Pages that run Markdown, gemtext, HTML, JS, CSS — or write a page in Hoon and it computes its content: commands, state, and dependencies on other pages, with live preview in the editor.
  • Knowledge & AI memory A private, tagged note store with history and trash. Browse it at /know; your agents read and write the same store over MCP, served by the ship itself.
  • Search everything A full-text catalog over your pages and the ships you follow — from the app or the web omnibar.
  • A real filesystem Mount your pages as local files with lattice-fs. A cold mount warms in one round-trip, so grep runs from RAM; edit in your own editor and saves round-trip safely.
  • Browse urb:// Fetch and read pages published by any ship, peer-to-peer over Urbit remote scry. The bytes come straight from the author.
  • Publish Anything you publish is instantly readable by anyone as urb://~you/that/path — and a page can also go clearweb-public, served over HTTP from your ship.
  • Follow & subscribe Follow ships to see what they publish; subscribe to a file for a live push when it changes. Discover publishers via %contacts; copy any remote file onto your own ship.
  • Web workspace A three-pane editor with tree, live preview, and drag-and-drop upload of files or whole directories. Installs as a PWA with the flat-green tile you are looking at.
  • Native everywhere Android, Linux, macOS, Windows from one Kotlin / Compose codebase, fully themeable, with bundled JRE on desktop.

What it isn't

  • Not a host. Bring your own ship — yours, a friend's, or a hosted one.
  • Not the HTTP web. Pages are Urbit-native (urb://~ship/path) and move between ships over remote scry, not over DNS and HTTP.
  • Not on the app stores. Sideload the APK and desktop installers from GitHub Releases.
  • Desktop builds are unsigned for now — Gatekeeper / SmartScreen will warn on first launch and you have to bypass it.

Privacy

The only server lattice talks to is your ship.

No analytics, no third-party SDKs, no telemetry. The app speaks to the Urbit ship you point it at — that's the entire network surface. Your session never leaves the device unencrypted: lattice refuses to send your +code or cookie in cleartext to a remote ship, and stores it owner-only at rest.

Everything you put in /pub is public by design — that's the whole point of a publishing tool. Nothing else on your ship is exposed.

Under the hood

On your ship, lattice runs as a nexus in the grubbery framework — pages and knowledge live as grubs in the vault, published pages answer remote scry, and the MCP knowledge tools are compiled into the same ball they serve. The native app is Kotlin + Compose Multiplatform over an authenticated HTTP API with live SSE updates; the filesystem client is Rust over a pluggable transport (HTTP or grubbery's local IPC). Hoon unit tests on the nexus, a JVM suite on the app, a 19-assertion ship-verified regression matrix on the filesystem client.
